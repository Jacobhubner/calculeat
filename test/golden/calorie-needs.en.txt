CalculEat / Articles / Calorie needs How to Calculate Your Calorie Needs? Complete Guide Your calorie needs depend on your basal metabolic rate (BMR), activity level and goal — weight loss, maintenance or muscle building. This guide explains step by step how to calculate your individual calorie needs using scientific methods. Step 1: Calculate your BMR BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate, basal metabolism) is the calories your body burns at complete rest — only to keep the heart, brain and organs running. It is the foundation for all calorie calculations. The most widely used and validated formula for most adults is Mifflin-St Jeor: Mifflin-St Jeor: Men: BMR = (9.99 × weight) + (6.25 × height) − (4.92 × age) + 5 Women: BMR = (9.99 × weight) + (6.25 × height) − (4.92 × age) − 161 Weight in kg, height in cm, age in years. Step 2: Calculate your TDEE with activity factor TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) is your total calorie need — BMR multiplied by your PAL factor (Physical Activity Level). Level Description PAL factor Sedentary Office or home work, little daily movement, no training 1.2 Lightly active Light exercise 1–3 days/week, e.g. walks, yoga or gym in spare time 1.375 Moderately active Regular exercise 3–5 days/week with moderate intensity, e.g. running or strength training 1.55 Very active Hard training almost every day (6–7 days/week) or physically active work 1.725 Extremely active Heavy physical work combined with daily intense training, e.g. elite athletes or construction workers who also train 1.9 Example: A person, 30 years old, 80 kg, 180 cm, trains 3–5 times/week (moderately active). BMR = (9.99 × 80) + (6.25 × 180) − (4.92 × 30) + 5 = 1,895 kcal TDEE = 1,895 × 1.55 = 2,937 kcal/day Step 3: Adjust for your goal Weight loss (cut) Eat 300–500 kcal below your TDEE. That gives about 0.3–0.5 kg of weight loss per week — slow enough to preserve muscle mass. Combine with adequate protein (1.6–2.2 g/kg) for best results. Muscle building (bulk) Eat 200–400 kcal above your TDEE. That creates a small surplus for muscle growth with minimal fat gain. More than 500 kcal/day in surplus often leads to unnecessary fat storage. Maintenance Match your TDEE. Great during rest periods, rehabilitation or to stabilise weight after a longer phase. Why doesn't the calculator always match? TDEE calculators provide an estimate with a margin of error of typically ±10–15%. Factors that affect the real value: Muscle mass (more muscle = higher BMR) Thyroid hormones and other hormonal balance Sleep quality Thermic effect of food (proteins require more energy to digest) Adaptive thermogenesis (body lowers metabolism during prolonged calorie deficit) Practical tip: Use the calculator as a starting point.
