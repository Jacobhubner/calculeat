Articles Bulk and cut Bulk and Cut — Calories for Muscle Building and Fat Loss Bulk and cut are the two phases in a classic training cycle. During a bulk you eat in a calorie surplus to maximize muscle building. During a cut you eat in a calorie deficit to lose fat and preserve the muscle mass you've built. The key is knowing exactly how large a surplus and deficit to aim for. Bulk — muscle building During a bulk you eat more calories than you burn. This creates an anabolic environment where the body can afford to build muscle tissue. Without a calorie surplus, muscle building is possible but considerably slower. Lean bulk (recommended) 200–400 kcal/day above TDEE. Optimal balance between muscle growth and minimal fat gain. Expected pace: approx. 0.2–0.4 kg per week (of which a large proportion is muscle in the first months). Aggressive bulk 500–1000+ kcal/day above TDEE. Faster weight gain but a large proportion is fat. Works for hardgainers and beginners but for experienced athletes mostly results in fat storage that needs to be cut away again. Cut — fat loss During a cut you eat below your TDEE to lose fat, with the goal of preserving maximum muscle mass. It requires discipline, adequate protein and strength training. Sustainable cut 300–500 kcal/day below TDEE. Recommended pace for most people. Gives 0.3–0.5 kg/week with good muscle mass preservation. Mini cut 500–750 kcal/day below TDEE, shorter period (4–8 weeks). More aggressive but time-limited — reduces the risk of adaptive thermogenesis. Requires high protein and strength training. Body Recomposition — the alternative Instead of separate bulk/cut phases you can eat around maintenance intake and slowly build muscle and lose fat . It's slower but avoids the yo-yo effect. Works best for: beginners, people with high body fat percentage, and those returning after a training break. Experienced athletes with low body fat get better results from separate phases. How do you know when to switch phase? End bulk when: Body fat percentage has become too high (approx. 15–18% for men, 25–30% for women). Strength plateaus despite calorie surplus. End cut when: You've reached the desired body fat percentage. Weight loss plateaus despite calorie deficit. Performance drops markedly.
