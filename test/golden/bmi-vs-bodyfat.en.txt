Articles BMI vs Body Fat BMI vs Body Fat — Which Measurement Is Most Accurate? BMI (Body Mass Index) is easy to calculate but only measures weight in relation to height — not how weight is distributed. Body fat measures the actual proportion of fat in the body and gives a more complete picture. Both have their uses, but they answer different questions. What does BMI measure? BMI is calculated as weight (kg) divided by height² (m²). The result is placed in one of four categories: underweight (below 18.5), normal weight (18.5–24.9), overweight (25–29.9) or obese (30+). The advantage of BMI is its simplicity — you only need weight and height. The disadvantage is that it doesn't distinguish between muscle mass and fat tissue. A well-trained person with high muscle mass can end up in the "overweight" category according to BMI despite low body fat percentage. What does body fat measure? Body fat is the proportion of your body weight that consists of fat tissue. It is measured with methods such as the US Navy method (based on body measurements), skinfold measurement, bioimpedance or DEXA (X-ray). DEXA is the gold standard but requires specialist equipment. Body fat gives a more precise picture of body composition — how much is muscle, fat, bone and fluid. This is particularly important for people with high muscle mass (athletes, powerlifters) where BMI systematically overestimates health risk. It's quick, free and requires no equipment. BMI is however misleading for: Muscular individuals (trained, athletes, powerlifters) Older people with low muscle mass but normal weight ("sarcopenic obesity") Pregnant women Children and adolescents (require age-specific reference values) Individuals with very low muscle mass and high body fat percentage ("skinny fat") Which goal should you use? Regardless of which measure you look at — BMI or body fat — your calorie goal is the practical tool for getting there. The calorie goal is based on your TDEE, not on BMI or body fat percentage per se. Calculate your TDEE to get a figure you can actually act on.
