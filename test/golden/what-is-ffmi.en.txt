Articles What is FFMI? What is FFMI? Fat-Free Mass Index Explained FFMI (Fat-Free Mass Index) measures your fat-free muscle mass relative to your height. Unlike BMI, FFMI is not affected by your body fat percentage — it is a pure measure of muscle development. FFMI is used to assess training level and, in research, to distinguish natural athletes from those who use performance-enhancing drugs. What is FFMI? Fat Free Mass Index (FFMI) is a measure that describes the amount of fat-free mass (muscles, skeleton, organs) relative to your height. It gives a fairer picture of muscle mass than weight alone, because it accounts for body height. How is FFMI calculated? FFMI = Fat-free mass (kg) / (Height (m))² Fat-free mass is calculated as: Total weight × (1 - Body fat% / 100) Practical application • Track muscle gains independent of body fat • Compare muscle mass between people of different heights • Assess genetic muscle potential ⚠ Important to know FFMI is affected by the accuracy of the body fat measurement. An inaccurate body fat measurement gives an inaccurate FFMI value. What is Normalized FFMI? Normaliserad FFMI is an adjustment of the standard FFMI value to compensate for differences in body height. Taller people naturally have higher FFMI values, so normalization makes it possible to compare muscle mass more fairly between people of different heights. How is Normalized FFMI calculated? Normalized FFMI = FFMI + 6.1 × (1.8 - Height (m)) The formula normalizes all values to a reference height of 1.8 meters (180 cm). Why is FFMI normalized? • Taller people naturally have higher FFMI values due to increased muscle mass relative to body height • Shorter people naturally have lower FFMI values • Normalization provides a fairer comparison between individuals Practical application • Compare muscle mass independent of body height • Assess whether muscle mass is within natural limits • Set realistic goals for muscle building ⚠ Important to know Just like FFMI, normalized FFMI is affected by the accuracy of the body fat measurement. An inaccurate measurement gives inaccurate values. How do you improve your FFMI? FFMI increases when you build muscle mass — it's that simple. In practice it requires a combination of consistent strength training, adequate protein intake (1.6–2.2 g/kg body weight) and a slight calorie surplus (bulk). Important: reducing body fat percentage without building muscle does not improve FFMI — it remains unchanged. FFMI measures only fat-free mass, not how your body looks in the mirror.
