Activity Level and Energy Expenditure Explained PAL (Physical Activity Level) is the activity factor multiplied by BMR to give your TDEE — your actual calorie needs. MET (Metabolic Equivalent of Task) measures how intense a specific activity is relative to rest. Both are used to calculate energy expenditure, but in different ways. What are PAL and MET? PAL och MET are both used to estimate energy expenditure — but in different ways. PAL – Physical Activity Level PAL is a multiplier that describes your total energy expenditure relative to your BMR/RMR over a full day. TDEE = RMR × PAL PAL values account for all activity during a 24-hour period. MET – Metabolic Equivalent of Task MET is a measure of energy expenditure for a specific activity at a specific time. 1 MET = energy at rest (≈ 1 kcal/kg/hour) PAL vs MET – The difference PAL ✓ Overview of an entire day ✓ Used to calculate TDEE ✓ One value per day (1.2–2.5) ✓ Accounts for all activity MET ✓ Specific activity at a point in time ✓ Used to calculate calories for a single activity ✓ Varies per activity (1–20+ MET) ✓ Measures intensity of an activity A rough rule of thumb The relationship between steps per day and a reasonable estimate of PAL. Steps per day Reasonable PAL ≤ 5,000 ~1.2–1.3 (sedentary) 7,000–10,000 ~1.4–1.6 (moderate activity) > 10,000 ~1.6+ depending on pace and other activity 10,000 steps corresponds to approximately 6.5–8 kilometers. When to use what? Daily energy requirement → RMR × PAL Exercise session → MET × weight × time Correct way to combine MET MET describes the intensity of an activity. How MET should be used depends on whether your PAL value already includes exercise or only reflects daily activity. PAL includes exercise TDEE = RMR × PAL If the PAL value includes both daily activity and exercise, no additional calculation is needed. The energy expenditure from exercise is already included in PAL. PAL is based on daily life (no exercise) TDEE = RMR × PAL + RMR × (MET − 1) × time Here PAL represents only daily activity (NEAT), and exercise is added separately. MET − 1 is used because only the extra energy above rest should be added — resting expenditure is already included in RMR × PAL. Summary • Use PAL alone if it includes exercise • Use PAL + (MET − 1) if exercise is added separately • Never use full MET together with RMR × PAL, as this leads to double-counting of resting expenditure Choose the right PAL for your calorie goal The most common mistake in TDEE calculation is overestimating your activity level. If you train 3 days per week but sit still the rest of the day, you are probably "lightly active" (PAL 1.375) — not "moderately active" (PAL 1.55). A practical method: start with a conservative PAL value and increase if your weight drops faster than planned. It's easier to adjust up than to handle an excessively large calorie deficit.
