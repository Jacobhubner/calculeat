CalculEat / Articles / BMR vs TDEE BMR vs TDEE — What Is the Difference and Which Should You Use? BMR is your resting burn rate — the calories your body needs at complete rest. TDEE is what you actually burn over an entire day, including movement, exercise and digestion. This is an important distinction: you should always plan calories based on TDEE, never BMR. What is BMR? BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate) is the calories your body burns at complete rest — without movement, digestion or external activity. It is the energy that goes to keeping the heart, lungs, brain and organs running. BMR is calculated with formulas like Mifflin-St Jeor or Harris-Benedict, based on gender, age, weight and height. It is the starting point for all calorie calculations — but it is not the number you should eat at. What is TDEE? TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) is the calories you actually burn during a day — including all movement and activity. TDEE is calculated as: TDEE = BMR × Activity Factor (PAL) PAL (Physical Activity Level) ranges from 1.3 for sedentary to 2.1 or more for very active. TDEE includes planned exercise (EAT), spontaneous movement (NEAT) and the energy cost of digestion (TEF). Comparison: BMR vs TDEE Property BMR TDEE What is measured? Example: a person with BMR 1,700 kcal and TDEE 2,400 kcal who eats 1,700 kcal thinks they are at maintenance — but are actually in a 700 kcal deficit. This leads to rapid weight loss initially, but with significant muscle mass loss and metabolic adaptation. The body lowers BMR to save energy, making it harder to sustain weight loss. Right approach: calculate your TDEE and create a controlled deficit of — sufficient for sustainable weight loss without sacrificing muscle mass.
